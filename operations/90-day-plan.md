# First 90 days

Planning start: 2026-09-24. Audience: 0–7 families; parent-child dancing is the constant. Default capacity: one polished original song per week, awaiting owner confirmation. If production takes longer, stretch the schedule rather than reduce acceptance standards. No success or revenue target is invented from the current tiny baseline.

| Period | Work | Evidence required to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–7 | Confirm language, lead activity bands, markets, weekly hours and spend limit. Capture first-upload metrics when available. Finish rights/disclosure records. Approve cast and a finished sample for the next song. | Accepted sample, usable measurement setup, budget ceiling; no automatic release |
| Weeks 2–4 | Produce a consistent baseline block of up to three songs across family copy-play, freeze/play and familiar-song movement. Keep cast, thumbnail convention and quality stable. Log song differences. | Production hours/cost, full playback QA, mature per-video windows; topic comparisons remain exploratory |
| Weeks 5–10 | Choose ONE experiment based on the baseline: parent-child mirroring, cue clarity, or packaging. Preregister three matched pairs and counterbalance order. Keep each new song substantively distinct. | Same windows and traffic scope, adequate observations, intact guardrails; otherwise inconclusive |
| Weeks 11–13 | Review matured pairs, retain a provisional direction if supported, prepare a replication. A purposeful compilation or tiny Shorts pilot is optional only if capacity and evidence permit. | Documented decision and remaining uncertainty; no claim of causality from observational comparisons |

The final releases may not have full 28-day fallback data by day 90. Carry those experiments forward as pending, rather than report them as completed.

## Candidate briefs, not a committed upload queue

| Concept | Shared activity | Lead band | Learning purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clap & Copy | Parent claps a short pattern; child copies; roles reverse | 3–5 | Tests clarity and appeal of family mirroring |
| Freeze Together | Both dance, then hold a simple pose on a cue | 3–5 | Tests a comprehensible game loop |
| Twinkle Dance Party | Parents and children show gentle star/sky gestures to the approved song | 3–5 | Uses existing audio only after rights/audio review; not proof the song is finished |
| Stomp, Clap, Wave | Visible single-action verses followed by a shared chorus | 3–5 | Tests cue-demonstration alignment |
| Copy My Silly Move | Child leads; adult follows an easy choice of moves | 6–7 | Tests role reversal for the older activity band |
| Family Hello Dance | A greeting song with a simple seated or standing participation option | Adult selected, adaptable | Tests a recurring family occasion without developmental claims |

Future ideas can include counting, farm themes and seasonal celebrations, but a number/learning claim must be accurately delivered. Do not compare all pillars at once and declare the most-viewed one the causal winner.

## Weekly rhythm

Choose the brief and log the hypothesis; lock music; approve a short finished sample; produce and QA; release only within owner authorization; record costs and exact release state. At the planned review date, inspect matured data, update the results/cycle records and sync GitHub. One person can do this sequentially; no extra agents or paid tools are required.

The first 90-day success criteria are operational: a reusable accepted production style, a coherent small library within budget, trustworthy measurement, and at least one interpretable comparison or an honest inconclusive finding. Set audience-growth targets only after observing a meaningful baseline and available capacity.
