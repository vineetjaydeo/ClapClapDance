# Plan to reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualifying watch hours

Created 2026-09-24. Owner-selected goal: qualify for YouTube ad-revenue sharing through the long-form route. Audience: families with children aged 0–7; videos show parents and children dancing together.

**Working horizon: 26 weeks, 2026-09-28 to 2027-03-28. This is a planning target, not a forecast of acceptance or growth.** The owner confirmed **one polished original song/video per week, five hours of work per week, and $50–80 per month**. Use $50 as the normal monthly target and $80 as the ceiling. English remains provisional; no paid promotion is budgeted. The first two productions must establish whether the required creative quality fits these limits. Reforecast after four weeks of observations.

## 1. Define the finish line correctly

Reach both **1,000 current subscribers** and **4,000 qualifying public watch hours in the preceding 12 months**, then apply and pass YouTube's channel review. Use **YouTube Studio → Earn** for eligibility progress. General Analytics watch time is useful for diagnosis but is not interchangeable with Earn's qualifying total. Private, unlisted, deleted and ad-campaign watch time do not count; Shorts Feed viewing does not contribute to this route. [YPP requirements, S10](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)

The last inspected launch snapshot, on September 24, showed one public song and four subscribers. It is historical, not a fresh baseline for this plan. There is no verified Earn watch-hour baseline yet. Record both live Earn values at kickoff. Blank/unavailable values stay unknown. The model below deliberately starts from zero credited progress for planning; it does not assert that the channel has zero subscribers or hours.

## 2. Size both growth requirements

Four thousand hours = **240,000 watched minutes**. For a stable hypothetical content/traffic mix:

- Views needed for hours = `240,000 / average minutes watched per qualifying long-form view`.
- Views needed for subscribers = `1,000 × 1,000 / net subscribers added per 1,000 qualifying long-form views`.
- Views needed for both = the larger number, rounded up.

“Net subscribers per 1,000 views” is a planning ratio, not the probability that a viewer subscribes. Repeat views, unsubscribes and subscriptions from other surfaces affect it. When using actuals, match dates and exclude Shorts/paid activity where possible; if attribution is unavailable, label it a channel-level ratio with mixed sources. Never turn gross subscribers gained into net channel growth.

| Illustrative assumption | Average viewing | Net subscribers / 1,000 views | Views needed for hours | Views needed for subscribers | Views needed for both |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Working model | 1.5 min | 5 | 160,000 | 200,000 | **200,000** |
| Longer viewing only | 3 min | 5 | 80,000 | 200,000 | **200,000** |
| Better viewing and subscription yield | 2.5 min | 10 | 96,000 | 100,000 | **100,000** |

These rates are assumptions, not channel measurements or industry benchmarks. The working model produces 5,000 watch hours and 1,000 net subscribers across 200,000 qualifying views. Longer sessions can help hours while subscriber growth remains the limiting factor. Rates can deteriorate as reach expands; there is no fixed conversion law. Inputs and calculated outputs are preserved in the [planning model](../research/ypp-plan-model.json).

The working model averages approximately **7,693 views, 193 watch hours and 39 net subscribers per week** over 26 weeks. These are rounded planning averages, not minimum results expected from each new video. The growing library contributes alongside new uploads. Do not assume views are evenly distributed across songs or that later uploads have the same exposure time as earlier ones.

## 3. Build a library people recognize and choose again

Position the channel as **“dance together with your child.”** Keep a recognizable cast and musical identity. Each video delivers one clear family activity; the title, thumbnail and first scene should promise the same activity. Proposed description/closing line for adults: “Subscribe for more family dance songs.” Keep it brief, optional and at a natural ending.

Use 3–5 as the initial lead activity level, a proposal within the 0–7 family brand. Adults can choose listening/simple participation for younger children; add optional short challenges for ages 6–7 later. Account demographics cannot identify the age of the child watching. Do not build the plan around infant screen time or endless playback.

### Content formats

| Format | Proposed length | Role and release rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original family dance song | About 2–3 minutes, adjusted to the song | Main discovery and creative-development unit; one activity, memorable chorus, readable parent-child movement |
| Curated family dance session | About 10–15 minutes | Optional watch-depth pilot after eight accepted songs and a two-song release buffer exist; only within the same time/budget limits, without displacing the weekly original |
| Shorts | Deferred in the core plan | Additional editing and distribution would compete for the five-hour weekly allowance; no Shorts watch hours included in the 4,000-hour plan |

Lengths are editorial hypotheses, not requirements for recommendations or monetization. A session must earn its duration. Use only cleared channel-owned material, avoid repetitive repackaging and evaluate compilations against other compilations. Their longer duration does not guarantee longer viewing. YouTube assesses originality and substantive variation across the channel. [Monetization policy, S11](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

### First four briefs

| Release slot | Working concept | Family activity | Packaging direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Clap & Copy | Adult claps a simple pattern, child copies, then roles reverse | Parent and child visibly clapping together; short “Clap & Copy” cue |
| Week 2 | Freeze Together | Both dance, then pause in an easy pose | Clear paired freeze pose; title identifies a family freeze-dance game |
| Week 3 | Twinkle Family Dance | Simple star/sky gestures together | Shared reaching gesture; use existing track only after music/rights/quality review |
| Week 4 | Stomp, Clap, Wave | One readable movement per verse, shared chorus | Full-body pair with one identifiable action |

Keep the first four releases reasonably consistent in lead activity level, length and production style. Their song differences make topic comparisons exploratory. If the existing Twinkle track does not pass, substitute an original Family Hello Dance; do not release merely because an asset exists.

Subsequent concept pool: Animal Steps Together, Copy My Silly Move, Count Five Claps, Reach for the Stars, Rainy Day Dance, and Family Goodbye Dance. Finalize the sequence after the first review; verify any counting/learning claim in the actual song. Do not make superficial duplicates of the best performer.

### Production standard for every release

Lock the music; approve consistent characters; approve a 10–15 second finished sample; then produce the full video. Start understandable shared movement promptly, keep relevant hands/feet visible, align words and actions, and end naturally. Use the existing [production standard](production-standard.md), including full-output audio checks and separate technical/creative acceptance. The rejected Blender workflow remains retired under the existing project instructions.

## 4. Fit the plan into five hours and $50–80 per month

The core output target is **26 new original song videos in 26 weeks**, plus the existing public song. Compilations and Shorts are not needed for the model and are not promised additional output. There is no evidence yet that an accepted animation can be made in five hours at this cost; prove the process before treating the cadence as reliable.

At 52 releases per year, the budget averages roughly **$11.54–18.46 per song**, including subscription costs and failed attempts. Over 26 weeks the work allowance is **130 hours**, with approximately **$300–480** across six monthly budgets; actual billing dates control the cash ledger. A five-release calendar month has only $10–16 per release before reserves. These are spending envelopes, not verified vendor prices or a claim that current subscriptions fit.

| Monthly envelope | Core audio/video tools and generation | Rerun reserve | Total ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Normal target | Up to $40 | $10 | $50 |
| Higher-cost month | Up to $65 | $15 | $80 |

Count existing subscriptions used for this work inside the envelope; do not quietly exclude them as sunk costs. Before selecting a tool, check actual commercial-use terms, monthly credits, video length/resolution, accepted-output cost and export limitations. Use reusable character references, backgrounds, project templates and editorial structure while keeping each song and activity substantively different. Reusing the design system should reduce rework, not produce interchangeable videos. Keep music and video generation within one affordable workflow where feasible; do not assume three paid subscriptions fit.

| Weekly activity | Time allowance |
| --- | ---: |
| Mature-data review and next brief | 20 min |
| Lyrics/music selection, revision and lock | 45 min |
| Shot plan and consistent character references | 25 min |
| Generate, select and edit visuals | 130 min |
| Full playback/audio/movement QA | 35 min |
| Thumbnail, description, authorized release and logging | 25 min |
| Contingency | 20 min |
| **Total** | **300 min / 5 hours** |

This is a proposed time budget, not measured production speed. Track active work and waiting/render time separately. Account setup and deeper research use the same allowance, so the first week may become a setup/pilot week if a finished song cannot pass. Record any missed release and revise the calendar; do not label a motion proof as a polished upload.

For the first two songs, record accepted output, all failed generation attempts, labor and allocated cash. If either breaches the limits, simplify shots and reduce avoidable rework before the next attempt. If two consecutive attempts still cannot meet quality within five hours, explicitly flag the cadence as unproven and revise the production approach with the owner. Do not silently increase spending, assume extra unpaid work or lower the creative standard.

## 5. Give every release a discovery and subscription path

1. **Clear packaging:** activity name first, then “family dance” or the relevant song descriptor. Show the parent-child pair doing the promised move. Avoid keyword piles. Search phrases are candidates, not verified volume estimates.
2. **Coherent channel:** a clear parent-child promise and creator playlists for follow-along songs and longer family sessions. Made for Kids viewers have restricted saving features, so do not ask children to save playlists.
3. **Adult subscription reason:** consistent cast, useful recurring activities and a brief optional invitation for grown-ups. Do not ask for notification-bell actions, comments or end-screen clicks; those features are restricted on Made for Kids content. [Feature rules, S03](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
4. **Relevant adult discovery:** prepare a short, accurate activity description and a parent/teacher feedback prompt for each release. If the owner authorizes outreach, share only where welcomed and within community rules. Start with five opt-in adults for comprehension feedback, not views/subscription quotas. No messages are sent by this plan.
5. **Respond to real demand:** inspect actual Studio search terms and suggested-video context when available; make a useful next song for a demonstrated interest. An early spike is a candidate, not proof of a repeatable format.

Prepare the activity description during the packaging allowance. Optional adult feedback or extra-format work must also fit the same 300-minute week; it is not an additional unpaid marketing workload. Skip optional distribution work when the original song needs that time.

Do not allocate money to ads to buy qualifying watch hours. Do not use self-play, bought subscribers or engagement exchanges. No paid-distribution budget is assumed. Publishing time is a workflow choice until there is relevant audience evidence; YouTube does not establish a universal weekly upload or best-time requirement. [Discovery guidance, S06](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)

## 6. Review two primary KPIs each week

| Metric | Definition and source | Decision it informs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current subscribers | Current channel total in Studio/Earn; growth is the difference between dated snapshots | Remaining subscriber gap; never sum cumulative snapshots |
| Qualifying public watch hours | Trailing 12-month total shown in Earn | Remaining hours gap and actual eligibility; later decreases may reflect expiry/corrections, not negative viewing |

Supporting drivers: processed organic long-form views and average viewing duration for hours; net subscriber change and the explicitly scoped subscriber/view ratio for subscribers. Break out originals, compilations and Shorts. Inspect CTR by source and 30-second retention only to diagnose a specific problem. Use returning viewers directionally when available, never as an additive count of families.

Guardrails: **accepted creative quality/rights/policy status** and **actual work at or below five hours weekly, with spend normally at or below $50 and never above $80 monthly**. Log unsuccessful generation attempts and subscriptions too. Keep one polished original per week as the cadence; use saved effort for quality/buffer before adding formats.

Use [the weekly template](../templates/ypp-weekly-review.md). Preserve the [measurement protocol](measurement.md): 7 complete reporting days after publication, with the same preregistered 28-day fallback for every arm. Reporting delay is separate from the measurement window. Record data-source timezones and do not merge mismatched retention periods.

## 7. Choose the next change from the actual constraint

| Evidence, with adequate comparable observations | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Low exposure and too little CTR/retention data | Improve clarity of activity/packaging and build the baseline library; do not label the creative a failure |
| Meaningful impressions but weaker CTR in comparable sources | Prepare a clearer parent-child thumbnail/title; log any authorized edit and interpret before/after cautiously |
| Clicks followed by early exits | Inspect promise/opening match and cue visibility; choose H01 or H02 only after identifying the relevant defect/question |
| Solid viewing but weak subscriber growth | Strengthen the recurring family use case, recognizable series and adult-facing invitation; inspect where subscriptions actually arise |
| Subscriber goal ahead of watch hours | Improve engagement and test a useful curated session; preserve stopping points and avoid padding |
| Hours ahead of subscribers | Make more distinct entry-point songs and strengthen the reason to return; adding duration alone does not close the subscriber gap |
| Production repeatedly exceeds agreed capacity | Reduce scene complexity/rework and flag the cadence as unproven; discuss a changed approach rather than silently increasing time/cost or lowering acceptance criteria |

Only one formal design comparison runs at a time. Choose H01 (opening), H02 (cue visibility) or H03 (packaging) after the baseline. Preregister six comparable original uploads across three pairs, at the same activity level and similar duration, counterbalancing order. Use weeks 5–10 if ready; otherwise move the experiment. Compilation releases are excluded. Mature every arm under the same window before deciding. Never intentionally publish confusing or poor-quality controls.

The existing practical floors (200 views/video, 1,000 impressions for packaging) are not power calculations. Native title/thumbnail A/B tests are unavailable on Made for Kids videos; matched uploads remain observational. Record `inconclusive` when evidence is missing or mixed. [A/B eligibility, S05](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)

## 8. Prepare for acceptance while growing

Complete account eligibility (2-step verification, advanced features, no active Community Guidelines strikes, eligible location), retain evidence of music/visual rights, use accurate Made for Kids and required AI disclosures, and keep every upload coherent and substantively distinct. Review the channel's most-viewed/newest videos, major watch-time contributors and metadata before applying. Keep AdSense/account details private.

When Earn confirms both thresholds: Studio → Earn → Apply Now → accept current terms → link/create AdSense through Studio → await channel review. The growth plan does not guarantee approval. Recheck policy at application time, especially because the calendar extends beyond the terms update announced on the September 24 YPP page.
